Clinical ASR — Staged Correction Demo

Concept demonstration for transcriber-radrx. Shows what a staged, auditable, safe-by-construction correction pipeline would look like in a clinician's browser. Every word change is visible, tagged with the rule that produced it, and bounded by a safety gate that refuses to touch clinically load-bearing tokens.

How to read this page:
  • red strikethrough — text removed by a stage (previous version)
  • green bold — text added by this stage (new version)
  • dashed underline — protected token (numeric, dose, acronym, anatomy, drug name). Hover to see why. Rules are forbidden from modifying these.
  • rule:name — the rule that produced a change (provenance)

Example 1 — Silent dose corruption (unrecoverable)

Source: bakeoff_dense_audiollm_instructable.json, Granite-Speech 8B + instructable prompt, voice en_GB-alan-medium. This is the cycle 110 safety-critical finding that was caught only by per-sample inspection — the aggregate WER metric hid it under a headline 0.0925 WER on this voice.

Ground truth
Neoadjuvant chemoradiation consisted of 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions with concurrent capecitabine.
Clinical context: standard pre-operative rectal chemoradiation. Total dose 50.4 Gy, the internationally accepted prescription.
Stage 0 — Raw ASR output (Granite-Speech 8B, instructable prompt)
neoadjuvant chemoradiationchemo-radiation consisted of 50.4 Gy504 gy in 28 fractions with concurrent capecitabine
Raw WER vs ground truth: 0.231
Stage 1 — Whitespace / punctuation normalize
neoadjuvantNeoadjuvant chemo-radiation consisted of 504 gy in 28 fractions with concurrent capecitabine.
whitespace:sentence-case punct:trailing-period
Stage 2 — Phonetic & spelling corrector (RT vocabulary)
Neoadjuvant chemo-radiationchemoradiation consisted of 504 gy in 28 fractions with concurrent capecitabine.
vocab:hyphen-merge:chemo-radiation→chemoradiation (TG-263 canonical form)
⛔ SAFETY GATE — STAGE 3+ REJECTED. HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED.
The raw transcription contains a protected-token corruption that cannot be repaired downstream. The ground-truth dose value 50.4 Gy was transcribed by the ASR as 504 gy — the decimal point was dropped entirely. No phonetic, grammar, or terminology rule is permitted to touch a numeric dose value (safe-by-construction rule engine), and in any case the original decimal information is lost in the raw output and cannot be reconstructed without re-listening to the source audio.
Additionally: 504 Gy is flagged by the dose-plausibility bound (no single-fraction rectal prescription exceeds ~25 Gy; total prescriptions in this anatomical context do not exceed ~60 Gy). Even without the ground truth, this value would be rejected as clinically implausible.
Action: sample is withheld from downstream consumption and routed to a human reviewer with the original audio, the raw transcription, and the flagged token. The partially-corrected Stage 2 text is not released as a final transcription.

Example 2 — Mixed outcome: clean corrections + ambiguous Gy/GyE block

Source: bakeoff_dense_audiollm_baseline.json, Voxtral Mini 3B, voice en_GB-alan-medium, clean condition. This is a Voxtral Gy → gye mis-transcription from cycle 111's bake-off. Two rules succeed cleanly; one rule is blocked by the safe-by-construction engine because both Gy and GyE are valid protected clinical tokens with different meanings (photon Gray vs proton Gray-equivalent).

Ground truth
IMRT plan delivered 60 Gy in 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including parotids and cord.
Stage 0 — Raw ASR output (Voxtral Mini 3B)
IMRTImmit plan delivered 60 Gygye in 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including parotidsparatids and cord.
Raw WER vs ground truth: 0.158
Stage 1 — Whitespace / punctuation normalize
Immit plan delivered 60 gye in 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including paratids and cord.
punct:trailing-period
Stage 2 — Phonetic & spelling corrector
ImmitIMRT plan delivered 60 gye in 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including paratidsparotids and cord.
vocab:acronym-phonetic:immit→IMRT (TG-263, confidence 0.92)   vocab:anatomy-phonetic:paratids→parotids (TG-263, confidence 0.95)
Stage 3 — Grammar rules
(no change — sentence is well-formed)
No changes applied
⚠ Stage 4 — Terminology rule BLOCKED by safe-by-construction engine
A candidate rule terminology:unit:gye→Gy matched the token gye, but the rule engine refused to apply it.
Reason: the rule would modify a token that is ambiguous between two distinct protected clinical tokens:
  • Gy — Gray, the physical dose unit used in photon therapy
  • GyE (often lowercased in speech as "gee-why-ee" → written as gye) — Gray-equivalent, the biologically-effective dose unit used in proton and particle therapy, physical dose × RBE (~1.1)
Confusing these underdoses or overclaims biological effectiveness by the RBE factor — a clinically significant error specific to particle-therapy contexts. The engine cannot disambiguate without additional context (was the prescribed technique photon or proton?), and it is forbidden from guessing on clinical semantics.
Action: the token gye is highlighted for human review but not modified. The partial Stage 3 text is released downstream with the gye token flagged; a clinician reviewer can resolve the ambiguity from the treatment plan or the original audio.
Stage 5 — Final (human-review-gated release)
IMRT plan delivered 60 gye in 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including parotids and cord.
Post-correction WER (counting gye as unresolved): 0.053
Net gain from the pipeline: 0.158 → 0.053 (10.5 pp WER improvement), with 1 token explicitly flagged for human resolution rather than silently auto-corrected.

Example 3 — Clean phonetic correction (Gy → jai → Gy)

Source: bakeoff_dense_audiollm_baseline.json, Voxtral Mini 3B, voice en_US-lessac-high, clean condition. Voxtral transcribes Gy as jai — a phonetic substitution that happens to produce a non-word (not a valid clinical token). This is the most common Voxtral Gy failure mode observed in cycle 111, and it's also the happy path for the correction pipeline: because jai has no valid clinical interpretation, the rule engine can safely normalize it to Gy with high confidence, no ambiguity, no block. Contrast with Example 2 where gye collides with the valid proton-therapy token GyE and the engine correctly refuses.

Ground truth
IMRT plan delivered 60 Gy in 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including parotids and cord.
Stage 0 — Raw ASR output (Voxtral Mini 3B)
IMRTImmit plan delivered 60 Gyjai and 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARsOIRs including parotidsparitids and cord.
Raw WER vs ground truth: 0.263
Stage 1 — Whitespace / punctuation normalize
Immit plan delivered 60 jai and 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OIRs including paritids and cord.
punct:trailing-period
Stage 2 — Phonetic & spelling corrector
ImmitIMRT plan delivered 60 jaiGy and 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OIRsOARs including paritidsparotids and cord.
vocab:acronym-phonetic:immit→IMRT (TG-263, confidence 0.92)   vocab:unit-phonetic:jai→Gy (RT vocabulary, confidence 0.88, unambiguous — jai has no valid clinical interpretation)   vocab:acronym-phonetic:OIRs→OARs (TG-263, confidence 0.90)   vocab:anatomy-phonetic:paritids→parotids (TG-263, confidence 0.94)
Stage 3 — Grammar rules
IMRT plan delivered 60 Gy andin 30 fractions to the PTV sparing the OARs including parotids and cord.
grammar:prep-clinical:and→in (RT dictation corpus, preposition-in-dose-fractionation-phrase, confidence 0.97)
Note: this grammar rule only applies when the preceding token is a protected dose expression (N Gy) and the following token is a numeric fractionation count. The rule is narrowly scoped by protected-token context, so it cannot corrupt unrelated sentences where "and" is grammatically correct.
Stage 4 — Terminology standardization
(no change — all RT vocabulary already in canonical form)
No changes applied
Stage 5 — Final (released for downstream consumption)

Post-correction WER: 0.000 — identical to ground truth.
Net gain from the pipeline: 0.263 → 0.000 (26.3 pp WER improvement). Five separate rules contributed: one punctuation, three phonetic acronym/unit/anatomy corrections, one grammar preposition fix. Every change is visible, tagged, and auditable. No protected-token modifications; no safety gates triggered.

What this demo demonstrates

1. Auditable provenance

Every change is visible and tagged with the rule that produced it. A clinician can scan a whole transcription in seconds and any correction stands out immediately. If a correction looks wrong, the rule is named and can be audited, disabled, or refined.

2. Safe-by-construction, not safe-by-careful-authoring

The rule engine refuses to apply any rule that would modify a protected clinical token (numeric values, dose expressions, RT acronyms, drug names, anatomy terms). This is a property of the engine, not of the rule author remembering to check. Example 1 shows the engine correctly refusing to touch the corrupted 504 numeric; Example 2 shows it correctly refusing to disambiguate gye between Gy and GyE.

3. Unrecoverable failures are rejected, not hidden

Example 1 shows the pipeline recognizing that the raw ASR output contains an unrecoverable protected-token corruption, rejecting the sample from automated release, and routing it to human review instead. The partially-corrected intermediate text is never released as a clean final transcription — which is the failure mode that would have silently propagated a 10× lethal dose in the cycle 110 Granite-Speech 8B bake-off if there had been no human checking per-sample.

4. Recoverable failures are corrected with explicit tracking

Example 2 shows clean phonetic corrections (Immit → IMRT, paratids → parotids) improving WER from 0.158 to 0.053 while leaving the ambiguous gye token explicitly flagged rather than silently guessed at.

5. WER per stage tells you which layers earn their keep

A correction stage that doesn't reduce WER on average across the bake-off fixture set is adding complexity and risk without benefit, and should be removed or redesigned. The per-stage WER column in the pipeline report makes this directly measurable.
